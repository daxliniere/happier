---
title: App lock
description: Require a password before Happier shows anything on your devices, with email-based password reset.
---

The app lock asks for a password before Happier shows anything. The password belongs to your **account**:
once you turn the lock on for one device, it turns itself on, with the same password, on every other device
you sign in to. It is available in the desktop, Android and web apps.

## Turning it on

Open **Settings → App lock**.

1. Under **Email server**, enter the recovery email and the SMTP server Happier should use to send you a
   reset code if you forget the password (server, port, security, login and sender address). The recovery
   address and mail settings are stored on the server and shared by all instances of your account. Save
   them, then use **Send test email** to check the message arrives at that recovery address.
2. Choose a password, typing it twice. Any 4 to 30 characters work; a mix of
   upper and lower case letters, numbers and symbols is harder to guess.
3. Choose **Turn on app lock**.

On your other devices nothing needs to be set up: the lock turns on by itself the next time Happier starts
or comes to the foreground there, and the same password unlocks it. If a device is offline, it turns on when
it next connects.

Changing the password on one device changes it for all of them. A device that is offline keeps accepting
the old password until it next connects.

**Turn off app lock on this device only** (at the bottom of the page) turns the lock off for that one device
after you enter the password. Your other devices keep their lock, and that device does not turn it back on
by itself; use **Turn on** in the same page to do that.

Once it is on:

- Happier shows the lock screen every time it starts.
- It locks again after the time you choose in the background or minimised (immediately, 1, 5, 15 or
  60 minutes, or only when it starts). The default is 5 minutes.
- The lock button in the header, next to Automations and New session, locks it straight away.
- On Android you can unlock with your fingerprint.
- Notifications show no session titles, message text or request details, and have no quick-reply
  buttons, until you unlock.

## Forgotten password

On the lock screen, choose **Reset password?**. Happier emails a code to your recovery address; enter
it and choose a new password. If you can't reach that inbox, choose **Sign out**: this removes your
account from the device and turns the lock off, and you connect the device again from another device
or with your secret key.

## What it protects

While the lock is on, the device's stored sign-in is encrypted with a key that only the password (or
your fingerprint, or a reset code) can unlock. Uninstalling the app, or installing an older version
without the app lock, therefore leaves the device signed out rather than opening your sessions.

The relay stores the shared key in password-wrapped form, so that a new device can use the same password.
Anyone with access to your account can therefore fetch it and guess the password offline, and a short
password such as `1111` falls quickly. Passwords can be 4 to 30 characters; longer is stronger.

A device that has not turned the lock on yet (for example one that has not been opened since you enabled it)
keeps its sign-in in plain form until it does. Real enforcement would need the relay to issue sign-in tokens
that expire and can be revoked per device, which Happier does not do today.

The lock is meant to keep colleagues and other people using the same computer out of your sessions.
It does not encrypt everything Happier caches on disk, so it is not a defence against someone who can
copy and analyse the device's files.
