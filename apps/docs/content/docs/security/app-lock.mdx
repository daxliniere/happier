---
title: App lock
description: Require a password before Happier shows anything on a device, with email-based password reset.
---

The app lock asks for a password before Happier shows anything on **one device**. It is a
per-device setting: turning it on for a shared computer does not affect your phone or your own laptop.
It is available in the desktop, Android and web apps.

## Turning it on

Open **Settings → App lock**.

1. Under **Email server**, enter the SMTP server Happier should use to send you a reset code if you
   forget the password (server, port, security, login and sender address), save it, and use **Send test
   email** to check it arrives. This is shared by all your devices.
2. Enter a recovery email and choose a password, typing it twice. Any 4 to 30 characters work; a mix of
   upper and lower case letters, numbers and symbols is harder to guess.
3. Choose **Turn on app lock**.

Once it is on:

- Happier shows the lock screen every time it starts.
- It locks again after the time you choose in the background or minimised (immediately, 1, 5, 15 or
  60 minutes, or only when it starts). The default is 5 minutes.
- The lock button in the header, next to Automations and New session, locks it straight away.
- On Android you can unlock with your fingerprint.
- Notifications show no session titles, message text or request details, and have no quick-reply
  buttons, until you unlock.

## Forgotten password

On the lock screen, choose **Reset password?**. Happier emails a code to your recovery address; enter
it and choose a new password. If you can't reach that inbox, choose **Sign out**: this removes your
account from the device and turns the lock off, and you connect the device again from another device
or with your secret key.

## What it protects

While the lock is on, the device's stored sign-in is encrypted with a key that only the password (or
your fingerprint, or a reset code) can unlock. Uninstalling the app, or installing an older version
without the app lock, therefore leaves the device signed out rather than opening your sessions.

The lock is meant to keep colleagues and other people using the same computer out of your sessions.
It does not encrypt everything Happier caches on disk, so it is not a defence against someone who can
copy and analyse the device's files.
